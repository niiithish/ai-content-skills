# Hooks

Contents: what makes a hook work · patterns · anti-patterns · testing.

The hook is the first 1-3 seconds. Everything downstream is worthless if it fails. Write five, pick one.

## What makes a hook work

A hook earns the next three seconds by opening a loop the viewer needs closed. Three mechanics do that:

- **Curiosity gap.** A question posed but not yet answered.
- **Pattern interrupt.** Something that does not look like an ad — mid-action, mid-sentence, unpolished.
- **Self-recognition.** The viewer sees their own situation described precisely enough that it feels personal.

Specificity is what separates these from filler. "Someone asked if this survives the shower" works because a stranger's question is a real event. "Are you tired of jewellery that tarnishes?" fails because nobody talks that way and the viewer knows a pitch follows.

## Patterns

**Borrowed question.** "Someone asked me if..." / "I keep getting this comment..." Frames the video as an answer rather than a pitch. Pairs with a comment graphic composited in the edit and the creator pointing at it. Strongest available pattern for a product that attracts scepticism.

**Duration flex.** "Four months. Never taken it off." Fragmentary, concrete, and it implies the objection before the viewer voices it.

**Failure confession.** "I have thrown out four of these." Buys credibility by admitting waste. Requires the anti-confusion rule — the thrown-out items must be unmistakably not the product.

**Mid-action open.** No verbal hook; the first frame is already in motion. Works when the visual itself is arresting. Costs nothing in the voiceover, so the copy starts on beat two.

**Objection-first.** "I did not believe the waterproof thing either." Names the disbelief before the viewer does, which converts scepticism into curiosity.

**Specific-scenario.** "I wore this in the ocean in Mexico and it still looks like this." One verifiable scene beats any general claim.

**Contrarian.** "Stop buying gold-plated anything." Strong stopping power, higher risk of reading as bait if the payoff does not deliver in the next two seconds.

## Anti-patterns

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "Are you tired of..." | instantly recognisable as an ad |
| "Introducing the..." | brand-first, viewer-last |
| "I'm obsessed with..." | enthusiasm with no substance |
| "This changed my life" | unfalsifiable, therefore ignored |
| "POV: you..." | worn out, reads as template |
| Any question answerable with "no" | gives permission to scroll |

## Testing

Write five hooks against different mechanics, not five rewordings of one. Then check each: does it survive being read flat with no music, is it specific enough that only this product fits, and does it open a loop the rest of the script actually closes.

If the hook promises something the script does not pay off, the retention curve dies at the pivot rather than the hook. Fix the pairing, not the hook alone.
