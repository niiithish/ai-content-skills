# Story structure: hooks, beats and where the selling goes

Measured from nine winning song ads: Resilia, Rise mushroom coffee, a teen skincare brand, two dog supplements, and a mushroom blend, 1:14 to 10:04 long, all 3D feature-animation, 9:16. Plus Starpop's mattress ad (4.95 ROAS on a $554 AOV). A song ad is a **video sales letter that is sung**: one character sings the worst chapter of their life in the first person, the product arrives around the middle, and the hard pitch takes the last 10–15%.

## Length tiers

| Tier | Length | Sung words | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short | 60–100 s | 150–250 | A symptom callout, then product and offer. Cheap test of a new product or angle |
| Mid | 3–5 min | 450–800 | The workhorse: the whole journey, compressed |
| Long VSL | 7–10 min | 1,000–1,500 | The Resilia format, for considered buys. Stretch the pain and the climb, not the pitch |

Length comes from the words: sung words ÷ 2.5 = seconds of vocals (`song.py lyrics` counts them).

## Sub-avatar first

Write one card before any lyric: age, ethnicity, relationship, where they live, the closest person in their life, the private fear, the body symptom and **what that symptom costs them emotionally** (love, respect, being seen). The pain in the song is emotional; the symptom only matters because of what it costs. Variants are new sub-avatars with every beat rewritten, not word swaps.

## Hooks

Sung from the first second, with no instrumental intro. The shape every winner uses:

> **a shameful or painful moment involving the closest person** + **a twist that makes it worse** + (optional) **a tease of the payoff or "the one thing"**

| Archetype | Example from the references | Why it holds |
|---|---|---|
| Twist betrayal | "My husband didn't cheat on me with a younger woman. He cheated on me with my 46-year-old sister." | Sets up the expected story, then flips it into something worse. It also plants the product: the older sister looks younger |
| Everyone knew | "My whole crew knew my wife was cheating for two years. Nobody told me." | Public humiliation on top of the betrayal |
| Guilty expert | "I held my Frenchie while she died, with the same hands that wrote the prescription that killed her." | An authority admits failure, which breaks trust in the default fix |
| Revenge preview | "…at our daughter's wedding… just wait till you see the look on his face." | An open loop: people stay to see the payoff |
| Dreaded event | "I've been dreading this email… my high school reunion." | A deadline plus comparison with peers |
| Lost identity | "It started on my cheeks the year I turned 15, and I lost the girl I used to be." | Grief and nostalgia |
| Odd intimate detail | "She kissed my son on the mouth… and me on the forehead." | A small, strange rejection |
| Pet's point of view | "Dad stopped ignoring my scratching." | Guilt and cuteness |
| Symptom callout (short tier) | "If your socks leave marks around your ankles…" | Self-identification, listicle style |
| Innuendo payoff | "Oh my God, you smell amazing." "That's what she said." (a scaled perfume ad) | A double meaning carries it; nothing explicit |

Write three hooks from different archetypes, pick one, and write two more as A/B variants. Each hook escalates at least twice within its first few lines (v2 of the Mysa ad: "left me for my best friend", then "after my chemo", then "'cause I'm the one who told her"). The first frame must show the persona and the conflict at once; the format's thumbstop is low (2% on Starpop's winner), so the people who stay must be the buyer.

## The beat map (mid tier; stretch or squeeze by percentage)

| # | Section | Story beat | % of runtime | Music |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open | The hook: moment, twist, tease | 0–5% | Sparse, voice first |
| 2 | Spoken pivot | "So here's what happened." (three winners use exactly this line) | ~5% | The band enters |
| 3 | Verse 1 | A few lines of the ordinary world, then the humiliation scene in detail, the insult in another character's mouth | 5–15% | Low, intimate |
| 4 | Refrain 1 | The insult or wound becomes the refrain line | ~15% | First lift, choir answers |
| 5 | Verse 2 | Worse: everyone knew, or the overheard conversation | 15–22% | Tension builds |
| 6 | Pre-chorus | Frozen: she smiles through it | 22–25% | A held note |
| 7 | Verse 3 | The mirror: the hero believes the insult (body specifics) | 25–30% | Quiet again |
| 8 | Verse 4, list cadence | The failed fixes: products, diets, friends' advice, a doctor who says "it's just age". Rock bottom | 30–38% | Driving list, then a stop |
| 9 | Bridge | The mentor arrives: close family plus a credential, the finished version of the hero | 38–45% | Drums drop out, a new instrument, warmth |
| 10 | Bridge 2 | **The reframe**: "It's not X, it's Y." The blame moves from the hero to a hidden cause | 45–52% | Revelatory, rising |
| 11 | Product verse | The product named, one line per ingredient and its job | 50–58% | Key lifts to major, rhythmic |
| 12 | Doubt | The hero voices the viewer's objection; the mentor pushes back | 58–62% | Stop-time, then release |
| 13 | Climb | "Week one… week two… week three…": each week one small win **someone else notices** | 62–78% | Double-time feel, energy rising |
| 14 | Climax | Vindication: same place, same people, the opening insult reversed, the biggest vocal | 78–86% | Full band and choir, the peak |
| 15 | Refrain flip + converts | The refrain returns with its meaning flipped; a cousin orders, a friend asks | 86–88% | Joyful |
| 16 | Outro to the viewer | "Maybe you…" mirror, then the product, offer, guarantee, CTA | 88–97% | Drops to piano, intimate |
| 17 | Tag | The hook called back with its payoff | 97–100% | A cappella, then the instrumental tail (2–5 s) under the end card |

Where the winners put the turns (share of runtime): mentor 17–45%, product named 31–54%, hard pitch 85–91%.

## Rules the winners never break

1. **Pain first**, for 30–50% of the runtime. No product, no brand.
2. **The mentor comes before the product**, and is close (mama, brother, grandmother, cousin, an old dog) with a credential (a nurse for 30 years, runs a clinic, a vet).
3. **The reframe comes before the product.** "That's not fat, that's waste." "It's not your marriage, it's what your body builds with." Once the viewer accepts the reframe, the product is the only logical answer: this is the unique mechanism.
4. **The product enters as a verse**, one ingredient or fact per line, each with a job. The rhythm makes the spec sheet catchy. A variation that works: the ingredient sings its own verse in the first person ("I'm cleavers, I clean the clogged drains in your legs").
5. **The hero doubts it out loud** ("There's no way softgels fix four years", "I'm not taking what SHE takes").
6. **The climb is week by week**, and each win is visible to someone else.
7. **Vindication happens in front of the same people** and reverses the opening insult almost word for word ("He looks 60" becomes "You look incredible, what did you do?").
8. **The pitch reuses proven lines:** "if you still see the button below, it's still available", the discount, the guarantee, "after six weeks you won't need it", and a last line that calls back the hook.
9. **Stories get cloned.** Rise re-skinned Resilia's best-man story almost line for line, changing only the mentor's credential and the product. Once a story wins, reuse its skeleton on new products and sub-avatars.

## How far to push it

- Controversy belongs to the **drama**: cheating, betrayal, revenge, a family secret, an ex at the wedding. It does not belong in **health claims** or body shaming aimed at the viewer.
- **Insults come from other characters.** The hero never insults themselves and the song never tells the viewer "your belly hangs over your belt". Resilia's ads were banned when they did; the Rise clone hedged every claim ("might not be just a weight problem", "support", "around week three") and kept running. Hedge like Rise.
- Sexual health: Meta needs 18+ targeting and a health focus rather than pleasure. The confidence shows through the story (new sheets, a kiss by the car, "I didn't brace"), never explicit scenes.
- Serious illness (cancer, chemo) is the strongest and riskiest element: always write a variant without it.
- Claims stay inside the product page's own words, with no percentages the client hasn't confirmed. Flag each claim for the client. Label the drama as a dramatization if the platform or client requires it.
