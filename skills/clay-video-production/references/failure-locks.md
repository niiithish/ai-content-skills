# Failure locks: claymation

Known ways Nano Banana Pro stills and Flow Omni clips go wrong in claymation, with the fix that worked (mostly from the Mysa "Hidden Cause" VSL, Calvin video-7). Check new prompts against this list, and read it with the shared list in `video-production/references/failure-locks.md`. Add a row whenever a fix works that isn't here yet.

## Look

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Flat, toy-like "Pingu" characters, cold blue-tile sets, even light (the client reference's look; the user rejected it as a target) | "Claymation" as a style word only | Describe the material, eyes, light and palette physically (`scene-still.md` rendering sentence); "Pingu-style simple flat penguins with tiny dot eyes", "cold blue or fluorescent light", "flat even lighting" in the negatives; compare against `look/`. |
| Smooth CGI or vinyl-toy render | No surface detail asked for | Fingerprints, thumb-pressed edges, tool marks, slight asymmetry, matte surface; "smooth glossy 3D CGI render", "plastic toy sheen, vinyl figure" in the negatives. |
| Real feathers or fur on animal puppets | Animal named without material | "Sculpted from clay"; "real feathers, realistic birds" in the negatives. |

## Stills

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The clay product reads as a real plastic bottle (Mysa bottle sheet v1) | "Clay version" stated, but the shape stayed machined | Hand-rolled clay, deep thumbprints and whorls everywhere, uneven bulging walls, soft edges, separate hand-pressed cap with uneven ribs, zero shine; print painted on the front face only (`sheets.md`). |
| Product in a scene with a wraparound coloured label or a smooth cap (video-7 52) | The product sheet wasn't passed, or the scene described the pack | Pass the clay product sheet in every still with the product and quote its print; fix a wrong still by editing it with the sheet as the second ingredient. |
| A prop that "doesn't make sense" (video-7 3: a drawer knob on the side panel) | Real-world object logic not stated | Shoot the object square-on and state where its parts are ("one centre knob on the drawer front"); things face whoever uses them. |
| A metaphor prop nobody understands (video-7 5: a brass timer dial with a 0–90 gauge plus a second clock) | Several symbolic objects, or one complex one | ONE everyday object whose meaning reads instantly, its words painted on it ("90 SECONDS" on a stopwatch dial); "two clocks or timers", "a gauge or meter" in the negatives. |
| Hand-held product far too big (video-7 6: a tube) | Size never stated | Real size against the character ("a tube about the length of her face, held in one flipper"); fix with an edit of the still. |
| An older character comes back young | Age unstated in close-ups | "Clearly in her late 50s, not young: heavy lids, laugh-line creases at the eye corners, exactly like the sheet portrait." |
| A white unlabelled product, or blank papers | No text quoted | Quote short real-looking text for every surface that carries print in real life. |

## Clips

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Too much going on": the hand keeps changing, extra capsules (video-7 clip 58 v1) | The still already showed the result (capsules in the flipper), so the model invented more action | The still shows the state BEFORE the action (edit the result out); the clip adds only that action; hands, product and camera locked; exact count at the end. |
| The set vanishes and comes back, things slide in from the edge (video-7 clip 4 v1) | A camera tilt on a no-character still: the model invents what's outside the frame | Keep the still's framing (at most an almost imperceptible push-in); "nothing enters or leaves the frame"; move one small thing only. |
| Pages disappear, a flower changes angle, stacks shift (video-7 clip 15, seen in the 1080p cut) | A busy scene with many similar loose props | Simplify the still: few, separate props held in the character's hands against a plain blurred background; pin each one in the clip prompt; camera locked; only the head and eyes move. |
| Characters drift toward CGI or live action mid-clip | Style not restated | "Animated on twos with the slightly stepped cadence and faint surface boil of real stop-motion. Never smooth CGI, never photoreal or live action: every character stays the same clay puppet from @image1 in every frame." |
