# PRODUCT "CONCEPT" AD TYPE: plan

(plan.py pdf writes this line: shot count, length, what it's timed to, average and longest shot.)

**Concept.** The format (voiceover ad, sung ad, talking head) and its voice or song, then the story in three or four sentences: whose story it is, what goes wrong, how the product fixes it, and the feeling at the end.

**Look.** The style and how it renders (faces, skin, hair, fabric), the light and its colour, and any visual device that tracks the story (a pain glow that grows, then fades from shot N).

**Camera.** How shots move (push-ins, tilt-ups, pull-outs, arcs, pans) and which stay locked; the cuts, naming the shots that get a special transition (whip pan, wipe, fade).

**Cast.** **Name** (role, age): hair, eyes, face, jewelry; then each outfit with the shots it's worn in: "nightgown (1–3, 24–27), everyday: sage cardigan, cream tee, jeans (4–23)". Only characters in two or more shots or speaking on camera. Then **Acting:** their energy, gesture size, posture and how they talk ("sly and slow, big eyebrow work, leans back, talks low and smooth"). Say if they are, or are not, an earlier video's characters.

**Sheets.** Characters first, with the count and outfits ("Characters, 7 sheets: mom ×3 (everyday, pajamas, clinic), husband ×2 (work, sleep), son, daughter"), then the environments that return many times (with what is in them), then props. Say which places and props get one hero still instead of a sheet, and end with "Extras, no sheet:" and the shots each is in.

**Blocking.** For each place where two or more sheet characters share shots: where each one is, pinned to the room (which side of the bed, which chair, which end of the table), and the side the camera stays on: "Bedroom: husband on the window side, wife on the lamp side; camera mostly at the foot of the bed, so he is frame-left." Delete this paragraph when no place has two.

**Light.** For each place with a sheet or hero still: the main light as a visible object in the room, the time of day, what rims hair and shoulders, and what stays dark: "Living room, evening: the brass floor lamp beside the sofa is the key, the side-table lamp rims them, the corners stay in warm shadow."

**On camera.** Who speaks or sings on camera (lip-sync) and in which shots; everything else is B-roll under the voiceover.

**End card.** The end card text and the shot it sits over, and the captions (template, which words). Both are added in the edit, never generated.

**Open questions.** What the brief doesn't settle, short enough to paste to the client. Delete this paragraph when there are none.
